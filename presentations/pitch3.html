Why this matters

AI is climbing a ladder of human-likeness

Text

Read at your own pace. Easy to re-check a sentence.

Voice

Feels like a person talking. Passes once, harder to re-check.

Face-to-face

Eye contact and expression add social cues of confidence and authority.

Each step feels more natural. The open question is whether it also makes people accept errors more easily. Tavus Research, “Griffin”, Oct 2026: 26 of 54 people on a one-minute video call thought an AI avatar was human (self-reported).

Research question

Does a more human-like delivery help people decide better, or only make the AI more persuasive?

H1 · correct explanations

Delivery barely changes accuracy

When the content is right, text, voice and face should lead to similar decisions.

H2 · erroneous explanations

More human-like, more errors followed

A voice, and even more a face, makes people adopt the mistake and answer the affected question wrong.

H3 · error detection

Fewer mistakes noticed

Afterwards, people in the voice and face conditions report the error less often than people who read text.

Method · difficulty

We need difficulty variance, not a single level

Too easy

Ceiling effect

Everyone spots the error; no variance left for modality to explain.

Target

Medium difficulty

Some errors caught, some missed; enough variance across people and items.

Also target

Diversified items

Different domains and error types, so no single item drives the result.

01  Difficulty must discriminate — but human labels are imprecise.
02  LLM-assigned difficulty is also inaccurate and biased — models reject harder items.
Method · material selection

A pretest is needed. We did not run one.

01Start from real model outputs. A real AI error that actually misled people is more valuable than a mistake we invented.
02Fix criteria before selection: prior knowledge required, position of the error, surface plausibility.
03Write the rationale for every easy / medium / hard label, and have a colleague review it.
No time for a human pretest before this presentation. Labels rely on empirical experience and stay provisional — measured detection rates are the next step.
Confounds

Search and calculators are excluded by instruction.

Stimulus quality

Quality is a confound, not just noise

Structural variance

Same prompt, different seeds: flicker, lip-sync, naturalness. Generation quality is not constant.

Directional bias

Polish raises credibility and lowers vigilance. Detection can drop for the wrong reason.

Fixed-effect fallacy

If hard items get worse clips, difficulty and quality are confounded. Materials are not fixed.

People are poor at detecting synthetic media, so they will not consciously discount quality differences.
Quality control

Minimum viable quality control

WE WILL

✓Log model, seed, prompt and settings.
✓Best-of-3 where possible.
✓One quick check: clarity, audio, lip-sync.
✓Keep correct and error versions visually similar.
✓Record how many takes were rejected.

WE WILL NOT CLAIM

✕A second rater or an agreement score.
✕Full quality matching across conditions.
✕A formal quality check with participants.

Quality may still co-vary with difficulty. We report generation and rejection patterns, and treat it as a limitation.

Literature · what the numbers mean

Two studies, one lesson

Study 1 · text chat

79.8% follow the wrong AI

Situation: 1,372 participants answer CRT-style questions in a text chat.
Task: Decide whether to follow the AI's answer.
Action: The AI is right on some trials, wrong on others; answers can be overridden.
Result: 79.8% follow the wrong AI; only 20.2% override; confidence rises 11.7 points.

Shaw & Nave 2026 · N=1,372
Study 2 · students

52.1% adopt wrong advice

Situation: 36 students answer eight questions with ChatGPT's advice.
Task: Decide whether to adopt or reject the advice.
Action: Half of the advice is wrong; half the students read a short AI-literacy text.
Result: 52.1% of wrong advice is adopted; the literacy text does not help (d=−0.25).

Puppart & Aru 2025 · N=36
Text alone already produces high error following. The open question is whether voice and face make it worse — and no study tests that with the same script.

Future work

From one-shot delivery to interaction

Current design

AI delivers a fixed explanation. User cannot ask follow-ups. No real-time video responses.

Interactive future

Users can probe, ask clarifying questions, and receive spoken or on-screen answers.

Open question: does the ability to follow up reduce error following, or does conversational scaffolding make the AI even more persuasive?

Open questions for interaction

Three things we want to test next

probeDoes the ability to ask follow-up questions reduce error following?
replayDoes replaying the audio, or only hearing it once, change detection?
qualityHow do we measure interaction quality itself as a factor?
These become the natural next experiments once the one-shot design has been run.
