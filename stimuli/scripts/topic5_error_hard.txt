Most food poisoning at home is caused not by food that looks or smells bad, but by bacteria you can't see. Bacteria multiply fastest at temperatures between 5°C and 60°C, often called the danger zone. In that range, their number can double every 20 minutes. Cold slows them down, and heat above about 70°C kills most of them.

That's why your fridge should be set to 4°C or lower. Many fridges are warmer than people think, especially near the door, so a cheap fridge thermometer is useful. A freezer should be at −18°C. Freezing stops bacteria from growing but doesn't kill them; once food thaws, they become active again. So thaw food in the fridge, not on the kitchen counter.

Perishable food, such as meat, fish, dairy, cooked rice, salads and leftovers, should not stay at room temperature for more than two hours in total. On hot days, when the air temperature is above 30°C, for example at a summer picnic or barbecue, that limit drops to one hour. After that, throw away food you plan to eat cold, even if it still looks and smells fine.

Leftovers should go into the fridge as soon as they've stopped steaming, within those two hours. You don't need to wait until they are completely cold. Divide large amounts into shallow containers so they cool quickly.

When you reheat leftovers, make sure they are steaming hot all the way through, about 75°C in the centre, and reheat them only once. Thorough reheating is a powerful safety step. At 75°C, both the bacteria and any toxins they produced while the food stood out are destroyed. So food that has been left out too long, such as cooked rice, can be made safe again as long as you reheat it until it is steaming hot all the way through.
